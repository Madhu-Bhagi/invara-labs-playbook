## What We Sell

Invara Labs sells accountable engineering outcomes, not developer headcount or allegiance to a technology stack.

Our launch portfolio contains three focused offers:

1. Engineering Excellence Assessment.
2. Modernisation Accelerator.
3. AI Engineering Adoption Sprint.

Each offer has a bounded entry point, defined evidence, named client responsibilities, and an explicit decision at the end. Platform Engineering Enablement and EEOS Implementation are expansion services; they are not marketed as standalone launch offers until customer demand validates them.

## Offer Selection

| If the buyer needs to... | Start with... |
|---|---|
| Understand engineering risk and decide what to do next | Engineering Excellence Assessment |
| Reduce risk in a legacy or constrained product through a practical first step | Modernisation Accelerator |
| Adopt AI-assisted engineering with useful controls and measurable outcomes | AI Engineering Adoption Sprint |

If the problem, sponsor, access, or desired outcome is unclear, begin with the Assessment. Do not sell a broad transformation before evidence supports one.

## 1. Engineering Excellence Assessment

### The buyer problem

Technology leaders see delivery delays, architecture concerns, recurring defects, operational risk, or inconsistent practices but lack an independent evidence base for prioritising action.

### Best fit

- A material engineering or investment decision is due within 90 days.
- Leadership needs an independent view before funding a larger change.
- The organisation can provide technical stakeholders, system context, and relevant artefacts.
- The buyer wants prioritised action rather than a generic maturity score.

### Intended outcome

Leaders receive a shared view of material engineering risks, evidence behind those findings, and a sequenced action plan they can approve, fund, or reject.

### Typical duration

1–2 weeks, depending on scope, system access, and stakeholder availability.

### Scope

The assessment is tailored to the buyer's decision and may examine:

- Architecture and system boundaries.
- Code-health indicators and maintainability risks.
- Delivery workflow and quality controls.
- Security, reliability, and operational concerns.
- Developer experience and knowledge concentration.
- AI-assisted engineering practices where relevant.

The proposal identifies the specific assessment lenses; it does not imply full coverage of every domain.

### How it works

1. Confirm the business decision, scope, stakeholders, and evidence.
2. Review selected artefacts, systems, and engineering workflows.
3. Interview accountable leaders and relevant practitioners.
4. Validate findings and distinguish evidence from assumptions.
5. Prioritise actions by business impact, risk, effort, and urgency.
6. Present the decision package and agree next steps.

### Deliverables

- Executive findings and decision summary.
- Evidence-linked risks and strengths.
- Architecture and code-health observations within agreed scope.
- Prioritised 30-, 60-, and 90-day recommendations.
- Leadership readout and question session.
- Optional proposal for a separately approved next phase.

### Client provides

- An accountable sponsor and one coordination contact.
- Timely access to agreed people, systems, repositories, and documentation.
- Known constraints, incidents, delivery data, and prior decisions.
- Factual review of draft findings without controlling independent conclusions.

### Not included

- Certification or a universal engineering maturity rating.
- Penetration testing, legal assurance, or formal compliance attestation.
- Full codebase review unless explicitly scoped.
- Guaranteed savings, delivery speed, or transformation outcomes.
- Implementation of recommendations.

### Commercial model

Fixed fee after a short qualification call and scope confirmation. Price depends on system breadth, evidence availability, stakeholder count, risk, and required senior attention.

### Call to action

> Book a 30-minute assessment-fit call to identify the decision, available evidence, and whether a focused assessment can help.

## 2. Modernisation Accelerator

### The buyer problem

A valuable product is constrained by unsupported technology, accumulated complexity, fragile architecture, slow change, or risky delivery. Leadership needs a modernisation path that protects ongoing business operations.

### Best fit

- The current system has a measurable business or engineering constraint.
- A rewrite is risky, unjustified, or insufficiently understood.
- The buyer can identify a bounded product area for discovery and a pilot.
- Product and engineering leaders can participate in trade-off decisions.

### Intended outcome

The client receives a defensible target direction, a sequenced migration path, and evidence from a reference slice or pilot before committing to a wider programme.

### Typical structure

- **Discovery and baseline:** 1–2 weeks.
- **Pilot or reference slice:** typically 2–6 weeks.
- **Expansion:** separately approved milestone phases when evidence supports them.

### Scope

Depending on the qualified problem, the accelerator may address:

- Application or framework modernisation.
- Architecture decomposition and modularity.
- Frontend platform, Angular, or Nx modernisation where current expertise fits.
- API, integration, data-flow, or deployment constraints.
- Testability, observability, security, and migration safety.
- Incremental replacement, coexistence, and rollback strategies.

Invara Labs recommends only technologies for which it can verify delivery capability or approve a specialist model.

### How it works

1. Establish business outcomes, constraints, and baseline evidence.
2. Map the current system, dependencies, risks, and decision history.
3. Compare viable modernisation options and trade-offs.
4. Define the target direction and sequenced migration plan.
5. Build or guide a bounded pilot with acceptance measures.
6. Review evidence and make a go, change, pause, or stop decision.
7. Hand over decisions, artefacts, learning, and next-phase options.

### Deliverables

- Current-state baseline and constraint summary.
- Target architecture and decision records.
- Prioritised modernisation roadmap.
- Risk register, coexistence approach, and rollback considerations.
- Pilot or reference-slice output when contracted.
- Validation findings and next-phase recommendation.
- Knowledge-transfer and handover package.

### Client provides

- Product and engineering sponsors with decision authority.
- Access to the selected system, delivery workflow, and responsible engineers.
- Business constraints, release windows, and operational requirements.
- A client team able to review, adopt, and own the result.

### Not included

- A default rewrite, cloud migration, microservices programme, or framework replacement.
- Unbounded remediation of every issue discovered.
- Production rollout without agreed release, security, and operational controls.
- Technologies or platforms outside verified delivery capability.
- Guaranteed cost reduction or delivery acceleration.

### Commercial model

Fixed-fee discovery followed by a separately priced pilot. Wider delivery uses milestone-based phases with acceptance criteria and explicit go/no-go decisions.

### Call to action

> Bring one constrained product area and the business consequence. We will determine whether a bounded modernisation discovery is the right first step.

## 3. AI Engineering Adoption Sprint

### The buyer problem

Engineering teams are already experimenting with AI, but usage is inconsistent, sensitive context may be exposed, outputs are not reliably verified, and leadership cannot show whether adoption improves outcomes.

### Best fit

- A named engineering team and sponsor are available for a pilot.
- The organisation has one or two concrete engineering use cases.
- Security, privacy, intellectual-property, and tool constraints can be reviewed.
- Leadership is willing to measure outcomes and stop uses that do not help.

### Intended outcome

The client leaves with a governed workflow for selected AI-assisted engineering tasks, a trained pilot team, validation evidence, and a decision about wider adoption.

### Typical duration

2–4 weeks for one team and a bounded set of use cases.

### Scope

The sprint may cover:

- Use-case selection and suitability.
- Context preparation and data-handling boundaries.
- Approved tool and workflow decisions supplied or authorised by the client.
- Human verification and review responsibilities.
- Prompt and output practices for the selected tasks.
- Pilot enablement, observation, and outcome measurement.
- Governance gaps and a prioritised adoption backlog.

### How it works

1. Select the team, problems, baseline, and success measures.
2. Identify security, privacy, legal, and policy constraints.
3. Design a bounded workflow with named human accountability.
4. Enable the pilot team and run selected use cases.
5. Review outputs, failures, effort, quality, and user feedback.
6. Decide what to adopt, revise, restrict, or stop.
7. Deliver the workflow, evidence, and next-step roadmap.

### Deliverables

- AI engineering use-case and risk assessment.
- Pilot workflow with roles and validation controls.
- Context and data-handling guidance for the agreed scope.
- Team enablement session and reusable working guidance.
- Pilot evidence and outcome summary.
- Adoption, remediation, and governance backlog.
- Leadership recommendation for scale, revision, or stop.

### Client provides

- A sponsor, pilot team, and accountable security or policy contacts.
- Approved access to tools, repositories, and non-prohibited context.
- Existing AI, security, privacy, legal, and engineering policies.
- Baseline information and time for pilot participation and feedback.

### Not included

- Legal, regulatory, privacy, or compliance certification.
- Procurement or endorsement of an AI vendor on the client's behalf.
- Autonomous engineering decisions or removal of human review.
- Production AI products, agents, RAG platforms, or MCP systems unless separately scoped.
- Guaranteed productivity, quality, or cost improvements.

### Commercial model

Fixed-fee sprint after tool, policy, team, and use-case qualification. Additional teams or product-AI implementations require separate scope and approval.

### Call to action

> Choose one team and one costly or inconsistent engineering workflow. We will assess whether a controlled AI pilot can produce useful evidence.

## Engagement Principles

Every proposal will:

- Begin with a business outcome, urgent decision, or material risk.
- Name assumptions, scope, exclusions, deliverables, client responsibilities, and acceptance criteria.
- Use paid discovery when uncertainty prevents responsible commitment.
- Separate evidence from recommendation and recommendation from implementation.
- Preserve human accountability for engineering and AI-assisted work.
- Include handover and capability transfer.
- Use change control when the requested outcome or scope changes.

## Engagement Path

```text
Relevant insight or introduction
        ↓
30-minute qualification call
        ↓
Paid assessment or focused sprint
        ↓
Evidence and decision
        ↓
Milestone expansion or advisory relationship
```

Free content and reusable assets support trust. Low-cost products, courses, fractional CTO services, platform ownership, and broad transformations are not launch commitments. Add them only after repeated buyer demand and delivery evidence justify the operational burden.

Use this catalogue in the first 10 qualified conversations. Record which offer the buyer understands, which outcome matters, which scope creates concern, what evidence they request, and whether the next step advances. Revise the catalogue from those findings before publishing fixed prices.
